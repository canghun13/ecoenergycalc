How Long Will a Propane Tank Run a Generator?

6 min read · October 2026

"How long will my tank last?" gets a lot of answers that start with the generator's size. Size matters less than you'd think. What decides runtime is how much power you actually draw, how much propane you really have, and, in winter, whether the tank can turn liquid into gas fast enough. Here are the numbers for the setups most people have.

For a typical 3,500 W dual-fuel portable (rated about 3,150 W on propane), which runs 10.5 hours on a 20-lb tank at half load. A 20-lb "grill tank" holds about 4.7 gallons.

If your house averages a quarter to a half of the generator's output, a 500-gallon tank covers about six to nine days of continuous running and a 250-gallon tank about three to four and a half — one reason 500 gallons is a common choice for standby generators, especially when the same tank also feeds a furnace or water heater.

A bigger generator isn't always better. At the same 4.5 kW load, a 9 kW unit burns about 1.0 gallon an hour and the 22 kW about 1.3, because the larger engine burns more just to keep running.

Why a Tank Can Stop Working Before It's Empty

Propane leaves the tank as vapor, boiling off the liquid with heat drawn from the surrounding air. In freezing weather that boil-off slows, and it slows further as the tank empties because less liquid touches the steel. Flame Engineering's chart for a 100-lb cylinder shows a full one supplying about 113,000 BTU an hour at 0°F and 167,000 at 20°F; half full, about 64,000 and 94,000.

A 22 kW standby at half load needs about 198,000 BTU an hour. A single 100-lb cylinder can't supply that in freezing weather, so the generator surges or shuts down with propane still in the tank. The fixes are more cylinders manifolded together or a bulk tank: Elliott Electric's generator sizing guide puts a 500-gallon tank at over 260,000 BTU an hour even at 0°F. Small cylinders on large portables hit the same limit; frost on the outside of the cylinder is the warning sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a 20 lb propane tank last on a generator?

About 10.5 hours for a typical 3,500 W dual-fuel portable at half load, about 16 hours at a light 800 W load, and about 6 hours at full load. A 20-lb tank holds roughly 4.7 gallons. In freezing weather a small cylinder may not vaporize propane fast enough at higher loads.

How many days will a 500 gallon propane tank run a generator?

For a 22 kW standby generator, about 9 days at 25% load, 6 days at half load and 3.4 days at full load, using 300 usable gallons (an 80% fill run down to 20%). At a quarter to half load, that's roughly six to nine days.

How long will a 100 lb propane tank run a whole house generator?

About 17 hours for a 22 kW standby at 25% load and 11 hours at half load. A 100-lb cylinder holds about 23.6 gallons, and in freezing weather one cylinder can't vaporize propane fast enough for a large standby unit at half load.

How much does it cost to run a whole house generator per day?

At $2.50 a gallon, a 22 kW standby generator costs about $84 a day on propane at 25% load and $130 at half load. On natural gas at $1.70 a therm, the same unit at half load costs about $78 a day.
