How Long Will 100 Gallons of Heating Oil Last? (Days by Temperature)

6 min read · October 2026

Ask a fuel dealer and you'll hear "it depends," which is true but not much use when the gauge is heading for 1/4 and the forecast says cold. It depends on two things you can pin down: how much oil your house burns in a normal year, and how cold it's going to be. Here are the numbers.

So the honest answer for a typical house is about three to five weeks, dropping to around two weeks in a hard freeze. The spread between houses matters as much as the weather: the same 100 gallons lasts the large house only about a third as long as the small one.

Why Cold Weather Burns Oil So Much Faster

Heat loss follows the gap between inside and outside, and the industry measures it in heating degree days: how far each day's average temperature falls below 65°F. A 45°F day is 20 degree days; a 25°F day is 40. Double the degree days, double the heating oil.

Hot water is the exception. If your boiler heats your water, it burns roughly a third to half a gallon a day for a family no matter the weather — which is why the tank still drops slowly in July, and why the cold-weather multiplier in the table is a little less than double.

Dealers turn this into a single number for your house, the K-factor: degree days per gallon. The typical house above has a K-factor of about 9; the small one about 16; the large one about 5. If your delivery ticket shows one, divide the degree days you expect by it to get gallons.

What Your Gauge Is Really Telling You

Before you count on 100 gallons, make sure you have them. A float gauge shows the oil's depth, not its volume, and the common 275-gallon tank has a rounded bottom that holds less per inch. On that tank:

  • 1/2 is about 137 gallons
  • 1/4 is about 58 gallons, not 69
  • 1/8 is about 22 gallons

A typical house at 35°F burns through 58 gallons in about two weeks. That's why "order at 1/4" is the standard advice — it's less of a cushion than it looks, and cold snaps are exactly when delivery waits get longer.

How Long a 500-Gallon Propane Tank Lasts

Propane tanks are filled to about 80% to leave room for expansion, so a "full" 500-gallon tank holds about 400 gallons. Propane gauges read percent of volume directly — no tank-shape correction needed. For homes heating and making hot water with propane:

That's 80% down to empty. Down to a 30% reorder point — where many suppliers ask will-call customers to order — take off about 40%: the 1,000-gallon home gets roughly 53 days at 35°F before it should call.

Don't Run It Dry

  • The bottom isn't usable. Some oil sits below the outlet, and older tanks collect sludge at the bottom that can clog the filter if it's drawn in.
  • Running out costs more than fuel. An oil burner that runs dry usually needs its line bled and a restart; a propane system that runs empty must be leak-checked before it's relit.
  • Order earlier before a cold snap. Everyone orders at once, and delivery waits stretch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many gallons of heating oil does a house use per day?

In winter, a typical oil-heated home burns roughly 2 to 6 gallons a day. A house that uses about 800 gallons a year burns around 2.7 gallons a day when it averages 45°F outside, 3.8 at 35°F and 5 at 25°F, including about 0.4 gallons for hot water. Small or well-insulated homes use less; large or drafty ones can top 9 gallons on a cold day.

How long does a full 275-gallon oil tank last?

A 'full' 275-gallon tank usually holds 240 to 250 gallons, and you should reorder at 1/4, about 58 gallons, which leaves roughly 185 gallons of working supply. For a home using 800 gallons a year, that's about seven weeks at a 35°F average and five weeks at 25°F.

How long will a 500-gallon propane tank last?

A 500-gallon tank is filled to about 80%, or 400 gallons. For a home that uses 1,000 gallons of propane a year for heat and hot water, that lasts about 85 days at a 35°F average outdoor temperature and about 66 days at 25°F, or roughly 53 and 41 days until it's down to a 30% reorder point.
