How Many Tons of Pellets (or Cords of Wood) Do I Need for Winter?

6 min read · October 2026

Ask on any wood-stove forum and you'll get answers from two tons to eight. They're all right for somebody: the amount depends on how much of the house the stove heats, how cold your winters are, and how well the house holds heat. These tables give a starting point for each, built on published heat values rather than anecdotes, so you can buy close to what you'll burn.

So a 2,000 sq ft home in a Chicago-type climate needs about 3 tons; the same house near Boston about 4.5, and near Minneapolis about 6. If the stove shares the job with a furnace or heat pump, scale down by the share it covers: a stove that heats the living area of a 2,000 sq ft house, roughly half the load, needs about half the tons.

Buy a little over the table. Pellets keep for years if stored dry, and running short in February means paying peak prices or switching back to propane at the worst time.

Three things change these numbers more than the stove does:

  • Moisture. The 20 million BTU figure is for wood dried to about 20% moisture. Green wood holds much less usable heat and burns poorly, so a cord of it goes much faster.
  • Species. Dense hardwoods like oak, hickory and sugar maple carry more heat per cord than pine, poplar or soft maple. Heat value tracks weight, and a cord of dense hardwood weighs far more than a cord of light softwood.
  • What "a cord" means. A full cord is 128 cubic feet. A face cord, rick or "truckload" is often a third of that, so check the dimensions before comparing prices.

At a 25°F day-and-night average, a stove heating 2,000 sq ft burns about a bag a day; in a 5°F cold snap, closer to a bag and a half. A drafty older house can burn noticeably more, a tight newer one less. If your stove goes through two or three bags a day in mild weather, it's either heating far more area than you think or losing heat somewhere worth fixing.

Is It Cheaper Than What You Heat With Now?

At $350 a ton, three tons of pellets is about $985 for a season. That's well below propane (about $1,230 for the same heat at $2.50 a gallon) or heating oil (about $1,160 at $3.80), but more than natural gas or a heat pump (about $765). Frequently Asked Questions

How many tons of pellets do I need to heat a 2,000 sq ft house?

About 3 tons (141 bags) a winter in a mixed climate like Chicago or Denver if the pellet stove is your only heat, about 4.5 tons in a cold climate like Boston and about 6 tons in a very cold one like Minneapolis. If the stove heats only part of the house, scale down by the share of heat it supplies.

How many bags of pellets does a pellet stove use per day?

For a stove heating 2,000 sq ft, roughly 0.7 bags a day at a 35°F average outdoor temperature, about one bag at 25°F and close to 1.5 bags at 5°F, using 40-lb bags. Smaller areas or tighter houses use less.

How many cords of wood do I need for winter?

For a 2,000 sq ft house heated only by an EPA-certified non-catalytic wood stove, about 3 cords in a mixed climate, 4.5 in a cold climate and 6 in a very cold one. A catalytic stove needs about 12% less. These are full cords of seasoned wood; a face cord is usually a third of a full cord.

How long does a ton of pellets last?

About 50 days at one bag a day, which is roughly what a stove heating 2,000 sq ft burns at a 25°F average outdoor temperature. In milder fall and spring weather a ton can last two to three months.
