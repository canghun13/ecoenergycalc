How Much Does It Cost to Heat a House? By Square Foot, Fuel and Climate

7 min read · October 2026

Most heating cost answers quote one national average and stop. The real number for your house moves with three things: how big it is, how cold your winters are, and what you burn. Change any one of them and the bill can double. These tables cover all three, using the same model as our heating cost by fuel calculator, so you can find a row close to your home and then refine it there.

Prices are national averages: natural gas $1.70 per therm, electricity 18¢ per kWh, propane $2.50 and heating oil $3.80 per gallon. Equipment is a typical existing system: an 80% efficient gas or propane furnace, an 85% oil boiler or furnace, electric baseboard, and an air-source heat pump. The cheapest option in each row is highlighted.

Climate is the biggest lever. The same 2,000 sq ft house needs about five times as much heat near Minneapolis as near Atlanta, so a $340 gas bill in the South becomes $1,700 up north. The fuel gap scales with it: in a very cold climate, heating with baseboard instead of gas costs about $2,500 more a year.

The heat pump column uses a seasonal efficiency (COP) of 2.5 in hot and mixed climates, 2.2 in cold and 2.0 in very cold, because heat pumps lose efficiency as the outdoor temperature drops. That's why they tie gas in milder places and fall behind it in the north at these prices.

  • South: cheap electricity (about 15¢) puts the heat pump ahead of gas.
  • Northeast: expensive electricity (about 25¢) and propane ($3.30) push propane and baseboard heat far behind, and heating oil comes in slightly below a heat pump. Gas still wins wherever there's a line to the street.
  • Midwest: cheap gas ($1.15 per therm) makes gas the clear winner, at about a third of the cost of baseboard heat.

Off the gas line, a pellet or wood stove is often the cheapest heat of all: at $350 a ton, pellets beat propane at $2.50 and oil at $3.80. The pellet and firewood cost calculator runs that comparison for your prices.

Why Your Bill Is Higher or Lower Than These

  • Furnace efficiency. A 95% condensing furnace cuts the gas column by about 16%: the 2,000 sq ft mixed-climate house drops from $765 to about $645.
  • Insulation and air leaks. These figures assume a typical existing home. A drafty older house can use considerably more; a tight, well-insulated one far less.
  • Ceiling height. Nine- and ten-foot ceilings add roughly 6% and 12% to annual heating (the calculator adjusts for this).
  • Thermostat. Every degree you set back over a full day saves around 1–3%. See our thermostat settings guide.
  • Heat pump backup heat. If the electric strips kick in often, a heat pump bill can look more like the baseboard column. The aux heat calculator shows what they're costing. If your backup is a gas or propane furnace instead, the dual-fuel switchover calculator finds the cheapest temperature to hand off.

The fastest check is your own bill: last winter's therms, gallons or kWh, times this year's price. To compare fuels for your exact size, ceiling height, climate and local prices, run the heating cost by fuel calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to heat a 2,000 sq ft house?

In a mixed climate like Chicago, Washington DC or Denver, about $765 a year with an 80% gas furnace or a heat pump, $1,160 with heating oil, $1,230 with propane and $1,900 with electric baseboard, at national average prices. In a cold climate like Boston the same house costs about $1,190 with gas and $2,950 with baseboard heat.

How much does it cost to heat a 1,500 sq ft house?

About $570 a year with gas or a heat pump in a mixed climate, $870 with heating oil, $920 with propane and $1,420 with electric baseboard. In a cold northern climate expect roughly 1.5 times those figures.

How much does it cost to heat a 3,000 sq ft house?

About $1,150 a year with gas or a heat pump in a mixed climate, $1,740 with heating oil, $1,840 with propane and $2,850 with electric baseboard. In a very cold climate a gas-heated 3,000 sq ft home runs about $2,550 a year.

How much does heating cost per month in winter?

January carries about a fifth of a northern home's yearly heating. For a 2,000 sq ft house in a mixed climate, that's about $160 in January with gas or a heat pump, $240 with oil, $255 with propane and $390 with baseboard heat. Spring and fall months cost a fraction of that.

What is the cheapest way to heat a house?

At national average prices, a gas furnace and a heat pump cost about the same in mild and mixed climates, and gas pulls ahead in cold ones. Propane, heating oil and electric baseboard cost 1.5 to 2.5 times as much. Local prices can flip the order: in the South, where electricity is cheap, a heat pump usually wins.
