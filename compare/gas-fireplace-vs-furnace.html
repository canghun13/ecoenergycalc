Gas Fireplace vs Furnace: Which Is Cheaper to Heat With?

7 min read · November 2026

A gas fireplace feels cheaper than the furnace because it heats the room you're sitting in. Whether it is depends on three numbers: how efficient the fireplace is, how efficient your main heat is, and how far you turn the house thermostat down while it runs. Most direct-vent fireplaces lose more heat up the vent than a modern furnace does, so per unit of heat they cost more. Zone heating can still pay off, but less often than people expect.

Per Unit of Heat, the Furnace Usually Wins

Of the gas a fireplace burns, the share that ends up heating your home is its fireplace efficiency (FE). Canada has required at least 50% for heating fireplaces made since 2020, and the EnerGuide label shows each model's rating; Natural Resources Canada notes some models are more than twice as efficient as others. A typical direct-vent fireplace lands around 65%, while a new furnace delivers 95% of its fuel as heat. Heat from that fireplace costs about 46% more than the same heat from a 95% furnace on the same fuel.

Vent-free fireplaces put nearly all their heat into the room, but they also put all their combustion products and water vapor there. They're limited in some states and cities, including California, and should never be the only heat in a tight, poorly ventilated house.

When Zone Heating Pays Off

The saving comes from turning the rest of the house down, not from the fireplace itself. With the thermostat 4°F lower for 4 hours a night, the furnace saves about 2% of the heating bill, $3 a month on a $150 bill. That rarely covers the extra cost of a 65% fireplace. The fireplace comes out ahead when:

  • Your main heat is electric baseboard or an electric furnace. Gas heat is far cheaper than resistance heat in most of the U.S.
  • The fireplace is vent-free or a high-efficiency insert and you heat one room for long stretches.
  • You turn the whole house down a lot, for example closing off most rooms and living in one for the evening.

Against a modern heat pump, a gas fireplace almost never saves money, except in very cold weather when the heat pump is leaning on its backup strips. See aux heat vs emergency heat for that case.

The Pilot Light Adds Up

A standing pilot burns around the clock whether the fireplace is on or not. Estimates vary widely: figures collected by BuildItSolar run from about 5 to 12 therms a month per pilot, while a Wisconsin utility calculator uses 2.6. At about 800 BTU an hour, a pilot uses roughly 5.8 therms a month, about $10 on natural gas or $16 on propane. Through the heating season, part of that heat warms the house. Over five warm months it's about $50 of gas, or $80 of propane, with nothing to show for it.

Canada now requires new fireplaces to shut off the pilot automatically, or limit a continuous pilot to seven days. In the U.S., many newer models use electronic ignition. If yours has a standing pilot, turning it off for the summer is one of the easiest savings in the house; follow the relight steps in the manual, and have it checked if it's hard to relight.

Comfort and Backup Heat

The real case for a gas fireplace is often comfort and resilience, not cost. A fireplace with a standing pilot or battery backup can still heat a room during a power outage, when the furnace blower can't run. If that's the reason you have one, the pilot cost is the price of that insurance. For outage planning, see how fast a house cools without power.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to run a gas fireplace or the furnace?

Usually the furnace, per unit of heat. A typical direct-vent fireplace turns about 65% of its gas into heat for the house, a new furnace 95%, so fireplace heat costs roughly 46% more. The fireplace only saves money if you turn the rest of the house down far enough, if your main heat is electric resistance, or if the fireplace is vent-free or high-efficiency.

How much does it cost to run a gas fireplace per hour?

About 51¢ an hour for a 30,000 BTU fireplace on natural gas at $1.70 a therm, and about 82¢ on propane at $2.50 a gallon. A 20,000 BTU unit costs about 34¢ or 55¢, and a 40,000 BTU unit about 68¢ or $1.09. Running on low costs less.

How much does a gas fireplace pilot light cost per month?

A standing pilot burning about 800 BTU an hour uses roughly 5.8 therms a month, about $10 on natural gas or $16 on propane. Estimates range from about 2.6 to 12 therms a month depending on the pilot size and how it's adjusted.

Should I turn off my gas fireplace pilot light in the summer?

Yes, if you won't use the fireplace. A standing pilot left on for five warm months burns about $50 of natural gas or $80 of propane. Follow the manual to shut it off and relight it in the fall, and have the fireplace serviced if the pilot is hard to relight.

Can a gas fireplace heat a whole house?

Rarely well. A 30,000 BTU fireplace at 65% efficiency delivers about 19,500 BTU an hour, which can keep an open main floor comfortable in mild weather but won't spread heat to closed-off rooms or keep up in a cold snap. It works best as zone heat for the room you're in, with the furnace set lower.
