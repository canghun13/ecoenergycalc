Gas vs Electric Water Heater Cost: Monthly, Yearly and Which Is Cheaper

9 min read · Updated October 2026

At national average rates, a standard gas tank water heater costs about $19 a month to run and a standard electric tank about $42, so gas is cheaper by roughly $275 a year. A heat pump water heater (about $16 a month) closes that gap, and in some regions beats gas. Enter your own rates to see where your home lands.

Understanding the Efficiency Gap

Standard electric resistance water heaters (Energy Factor approximately 0.95) convert 95% of electricity to hot water heat. Standard gas water heaters (Energy Factor approximately 0.60–0.67) lose significant heat up the flue exhaust. On raw efficiency alone, electric wins. But electricity costs more per BTU than gas in most U.S. markets — which is why gas water heating has historically been cheaper to operate despite lower efficiency. The comparison is fundamentally about fuel cost, not efficiency: at what electricity rate does electric water heating become cost-competitive with gas?

The Break-Even Electricity Rate

Heating 50 gallons a day by about 60°F takes roughly 9.1 million BTU a year — about 2,670 kWh or 91 therms of heat in the water. A 67%-efficient gas tank burns about 136 therms to deliver that ($231 a year at $1.70), while a 95%-efficient electric tank uses about 2,810 kWh ($506 a year at 18¢).

Two rules of thumb fall out of the efficiencies, and they work at any price:

  • Electric tank vs gas tank: electric resistance only wins if your electricity rate in cents is below about 4.8 × your gas price per therm. At $1.70 gas that's about 8¢/kWh, below the average residential rate in every U.S. state.
  • Heat pump water heater vs gas tank: the heat pump wins if your electricity rate in cents is below about 12.7 × your gas price per therm. At $1.70 gas that's about 22¢/kWh, which most of the South and Midwest are under and much of the Northeast and West are not.

Against a tankless gas unit (84% efficient) the heat pump line moves to about 10.2 × the gas price, or 17¢/kWh at $1.70 gas.

Should You Switch? It Depends What You Have Now

If you have an electric tank, a heat pump water heater is the clear upgrade: it cuts about $314 a year at 18¢/kWh, so a $500–$600 price premium pays back in roughly two years. Our heat pump water heater vs electric calculator runs that payback with your numbers.

If you have a gas tank, switching to a heat pump saves only about $40 a year at national averages, not enough to cover the cost of a new 240V circuit and a unit that costs several hundred dollars more. Replace like for like when it fails, or consider tankless gas if you have the gas line capacity. The exception is going fully electric so you can close the gas account and stop paying its fixed monthly customer charge, often $10–$25 a month on its own.

If you're building or have no gas line, a heat pump water heater is the cheapest to run in most of the country and avoids the cost of running gas and a flue.

Installation Considerations

Gas water heater replacement requires gas line, proper flue venting, and expansion tank in closed systems — installation runs $300–$600 on top of equipment. Electric requires a 240V/30A circuit. Heat pump water heaters need 700+ cubic feet of unconditioned or semi-conditioned air surrounding them (they extract heat from the air), and they exhaust cool, dry air — a benefit in warm climates and seasons, a slight heating penalty in winter in heated basements. They also make noise similar to a window AC unit at about 50 dB. Basement, garage, or utility room installation is typical and practical for most homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gas or electric water heater cheaper to run?

Gas, in almost every state. At national average rates a standard gas tank costs about $231 a year to run and a standard electric tank about $506. Electric resistance only wins where electricity costs less than about 4.8 times the gas price per therm in cents, roughly 8¢/kWh at $1.70 gas. A heat pump water heater, at about $192 a year, is the electric option that competes with gas.

How much does a gas vs electric water heater cost per month?

About $19 a month for a gas tank and $42 for an electric tank, for a household using 50 gallons of hot water a day at 18¢/kWh and $1.70 per therm. A gas tankless unit runs about $15 and a heat pump water heater about $16. Larger households using 70–80 gallons a day pay roughly 40–60% more.

Is a heat pump water heater worth the extra upfront cost?

If you're replacing an electric tank, yes: it saves about $314 a year at 18¢/kWh, so a $500–$600 premium pays back in about two years. If you're replacing a gas tank, usually not on running cost alone, since it saves only about $40 a year at national averages. It beats gas where electricity costs less than about 12.7 times the gas price per therm in cents, roughly 22¢/kWh at $1.70 gas.

Is a tankless gas water heater cheaper to run than a tank?

Yes. A tankless gas unit is roughly 84% efficient versus about 67% for a standard gas tank, because it doesn't keep a tank of water hot around the clock. That saves about $45 a year at national average prices, though tankless units commonly cost $1,200 or more installed versus about $800 for a tank, so payback takes about a decade.
