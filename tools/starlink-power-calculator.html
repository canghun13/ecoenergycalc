Solar Panels for 24/7 Starlink

To keep Starlink running every day from solar, the panels have to replace a full day's battery draw in the sun hours you get. A Standard kit on an inverter needs about 2.5 kWh a day; with 3.5 peak sun hours and 80% real-world output, that's roughly 880 watts of panels. A Mini on DC needs about 0.8 kWh a day and about 290 watts of panels. Size for winter sun, not summer, or the system will run short exactly when days are shortest.

Add battery for the nights and cloudy stretches: one full day of reserve for a Standard kit is about 2,750 Wh of battery; for the Mini, about 900 Wh. For whole-home solar sizing, see the solar panel count calculator and the battery storage calculator.

Ways to Cut Starlink's Power Draw

  • Set a sleep schedule in the app for overnight hours.
  • Clear snow by hand when you're on battery. The dish's snow-melt heating raises power use substantially.
  • Use DC power where the kit supports it instead of an inverter.
  • Turn off the inverter when nothing else needs AC; many inverters draw power even with no load.
  • Choose the Mini for camping, RVs and small off-grid cabins where its speed is enough.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much electricity does Starlink use per month?

Running 24 hours a day, a Starlink Mini at 25–40 watts uses about 18 to 29 kWh a month, a Standard Actuated dish at 50–75 watts about 36 to 54 kWh, and a Standard dish at 75–100 watts about 54 to 72 kWh. At 18¢ per kWh that's roughly $3 to $5, $6 to $10, and $10 to $13 a month.

How long will a 1000Wh battery run Starlink?

About 8 to 9 hours for a Standard kit running from the power station's AC outlet, and about 26 hours for a Starlink Mini on 12-volt DC. A Standard Actuated dish runs about 12 hours. Snow melting and startup raise the draw, so plan for less in winter.

How many solar panels do I need to run Starlink?

For a Standard kit running 24/7 through an inverter, about 900 watts of panels with 3.5 peak sun hours, plus about 2,750 Wh of battery for one cloudy day. A Starlink Mini on DC needs about 300 watts of panels and about 900 Wh of battery. In northern winters with 2–3 sun hours, plan on more panel.

Does Starlink use a lot of power?

Not on the grid: a Standard dish uses about as much as a 75–100 watt light bulb left on, roughly $10 to $13 a month at average U.S. rates. Off-grid, it's one of the larger loads in a small system because it runs all day and night, which is why the 25–40 watt Mini is popular for RVs and cabins.

Does Starlink sleep mode save power?

Yes. The Starlink app lets you schedule hours when the dish sleeps. Sleeping 8 hours a night cuts daily energy use by about a third, which on battery power means a third less capacity and solar needed.
