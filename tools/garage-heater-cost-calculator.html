Heating only during use adds 25% for warming the air, walls and tools each session. Vented gas and propane unit heaters 80%, electric heaters 100%, ductless heat pump COP 2.5 (2.0 below 30°F).

Heating Only When You Use It Is Usually Far Cheaper

The biggest decision isn't the fuel, it's whether the garage stays warm all week. Keeping an uninsulated 2-car garage at 50°F while it averages 30°F outside costs roughly $315 a month on propane in this calculator. Heating the same garage to 60°F for 8 hours a week costs about $28. A heater that runs only during use pays for warm-up time, but it skips the 160 hours a week nobody is in there.

Keep-warm heating makes sense mainly to protect something: water lines, a drain, paint and chemicals, a battery bank, or a workshop where glue and finishes need to cure. For that, 40°F is usually enough, and it costs about half what 50°F does.

Insulation Matters Most If You Heat All the Time

An uninsulated 2-car garage loses heat about three times as fast as a fully insulated one, mostly through the open ceiling and around the garage door. Insulating walls alone helps less than people expect when the ceiling and door are bare. If you plan to keep the space warm, insulate the ceiling and add an insulated door or a door insulation kit first; if you only heat it a few hours a week, insulation pays back slowly and a bigger heater is the cheaper fix.

Sizing the Heater

The size you need comes from the coldest typical night, not the average. To hold 60°F at 0°F outside, an uninsulated 2-car garage needs about 38,000 BTU an hour — a 45,000 BTU propane or natural gas unit heater. Fully insulated, the same garage needs about 13,000 BTU an hour, which a 4–5 kW, 240-volt electric heater can supply. A standard 1,500 W plug-in space heater puts out only about 5,100 BTU an hour, which is why it barely dents a cold, open garage.

Fuel and Safety

  • Propane or natural gas unit heater (vented): the usual choice for large or uninsulated garages. Needs a flue or sidewall vent and, for propane, a tank or cylinder hookup.
  • 240-volt electric heater: simple to install and clean, but electric resistance heat costs the most per BTU.
  • Ductless mini-split heat pump: the cheapest to run for keep-warm heating in an insulated garage, and it cools in summer, but the most expensive to buy.
  • Unvented propane or kerosene heaters put all their combustion moisture and exhaust into the space. They rust tools, can raise carbon monoxide and are not meant as a permanent heat source; use them only briefly with ventilation and a CO alarm.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to heat a garage per month?

For a 2-car garage at a 30°F outdoor average, heating to 60°F for 8 hours a week costs roughly $15–$45 a month depending on fuel and insulation. Keeping an uninsulated one at 50°F around the clock costs about $195 a month on natural gas, $315 on propane and $485 with electric heat; fully insulated, about $65, $105 and $160.

How many BTUs do I need to heat a 2 car garage?

To hold 60°F at 0°F outside, about 38,000 BTU an hour for an uninsulated attached 2-car garage with a 9-foot ceiling, 32,000 with insulated walls only, and 13,000 with walls, ceiling and door insulated. A 45,000 BTU unit heater covers the uninsulated case; a 4–5 kW electric heater covers the insulated one.

Is it cheaper to heat a garage with propane or electric?

Propane is cheaper than an electric resistance heater at typical prices — about $2.50 a gallon versus 18¢ a kWh, propane heat costs roughly two-thirds as much. Natural gas is cheaper still, and a ductless heat pump is about as cheap as gas, though it costs much more to install.

Can a space heater heat a garage?

A 1,500-watt plug-in heater gives about 5,100 BTU an hour. That's enough to take the edge off a small insulated garage, but an uninsulated 2-car garage in freezing weather can lose several times that, so the space never gets warm. Use a 240-volt electric or vented gas unit heater sized to the garage.
