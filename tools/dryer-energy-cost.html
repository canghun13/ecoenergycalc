Electric dryer cost = element wattage × cycle time. Heat pump dryers draw about 2,000W but run about 25% longer per load. Gas dryers burn about 22,000 BTU/hour (0.22 therms/hour) plus about 700W for the drum motor and controls. Real use varies with load size and how wet clothes leave the washer.

How Much Does a Dryer Cost to Run?

A standard electric dryer draws around 4,000–5,000 watts and runs 45–60 minutes per load. At the U.S. average electricity rate of 18¢/kWh, a 45-minute load costs roughly $0.54–$0.68, and an hour-long load up to $0.90. At 5 loads per week, that's about $140–$234 per year just for the dryer — making it one of the top 5 energy-consuming appliances in the typical home.

Gas dryers are cheaper to run in most regions. A gas dryer uses about 22,000 BTU/hour of natural gas (0.22 therms/hour) plus a small amount of electricity to run the motor and controls. At an average gas rate of $1.70/therm and 45-minute cycles, gas costs about $0.28 a load and the motor about $0.09, for a total near $0.38 — compared with $0.54–$0.68 for an electric dryer on the same cycle. Over a year at 5 loads a week, that is about $98 for gas against $140–$176 for electric.

Electric vs Gas Dryer: Annual Cost Comparison

At 5 loads per week, 45-minute cycles, 18¢/kWh electricity, $1.70/therm gas:

  • Standard electric (5,000W): 3.75 kWh/load → $0.68/load → $176/year
  • ENERGY STAR electric (4,000W): 3.0 kWh/load → $0.54/load → $140/year
  • Heat pump dryer (2,000W, ~56-minute cycle): 1.9 kWh/load → $0.34/load → $88/year
  • Gas dryer: 0.165 therms + 0.53 kWh/load → $0.38/load → $98/year

Heat pump dryers use roughly half the electricity of a standard electric dryer by recirculating warm air through a heat exchanger instead of venting it, even though each cycle runs longer. Against a 5,000W dryer they save about $88 a year at 5 loads a week; against an ENERGY STAR 4,000W model, about $53. With a $300–600 price premium, payback is roughly 4–12 years on electricity alone — fastest for big households and high electric rates. They need no outside vent, which can matter more than the savings in a condo or a closet laundry.

Gas and heat pump land within about $10 a year of each other at national average prices, so a household that already has a gas dryer has little reason to switch. Where they differ is local rates: at 25¢/kWh electricity and $1.15/therm gas, gas wins clearly; at 15¢ electricity and $1.95 gas, the heat pump does. What Affects Dryer Running Cost Most?

Unlike washing machines, where water temperature dominates the energy bill, dryer costs are driven almost entirely by three factors:

  • Cycle time — The single biggest variable. A 45-minute cycle uses 25% less energy than a 60-minute one. The most effective way to cut dryer costs is to spin clothes longer in the washer (reducing moisture content) and clean the lint filter before every load to maintain airflow.
  • Dryer wattage — Standard electric dryers range from 4,000–6,000 watts. ENERGY STAR models are typically 4,000–4,500W. Heat pump dryers run at 1,800–2,200W.
  • Load frequency — At $0.38–0.68 a load, cutting from 7 loads to 5 a week saves about $40–70 a year with no other changes.

Tips to Lower Your Dryer Energy Bill

  • Clean the lint filter every load — A clogged filter reduces airflow, extending drying time by 20–30%. This is free and takes 10 seconds.
  • Spin clothes longer in the washer — Higher spin speeds remove more water mechanically. Getting clothes from 70% to 50% moisture content before drying cuts dryer time significantly.
  • Dry full loads — Small loads use nearly as much energy as full loads. Combine where possible.
  • Use the moisture sensor setting — If your dryer has a sensor dry option, use it instead of timed dry. It stops the cycle when clothes are actually dry.
  • Dry similar fabrics together — Heavy towels and lightweight t-shirts have very different drying times. Mixing them means the lighter items over-dry while waiting for heavier ones.
  • Run dryer during off-peak hours — If you're on a time-of-use electricity plan, evening loads can cost 20–50% less per kWh.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to run a clothes dryer?

At 18¢/kWh, a standard 5,000-watt electric dryer costs about $0.68 for a 45-minute load and an ENERGY STAR 4,000-watt model about $0.54. A gas dryer costs about $0.38 a load at $1.70/therm, and a heat pump dryer about $0.34.

What is the average cost of a clothes dryer per year?

At 5 loads a week, a standard electric dryer costs about $176 a year, an ENERGY STAR electric model about $140, a gas dryer about $98 and a heat pump dryer about $88, using national average rates of 18¢/kWh and $1.70/therm.

Is it cheaper to run a gas or electric dryer?

Gas is about 45% cheaper per load than a standard electric dryer at national average prices: roughly $0.38 versus $0.68. A heat pump electric dryer closes most of that gap at about $0.34 a load. Local rates decide the winner, so compare with your own electricity and gas prices.

Is a heat pump dryer worth it?

It saves about $53–88 a year at 5 loads a week compared with a conventional electric dryer, so a $300–600 price premium pays back in roughly 4–12 years. It makes the most sense for large households, high electric rates, or homes where installing a dryer vent is difficult.
