What a Typical Display Costs

LED lights have made strings cheap to run: six 100-light LED strings use about 45 watts together, less than one incandescent string. In many yards today the biggest load is the inflatables. Two full-size inflatables at 100 watts each use more than four times as much as those six strings. The calculator's starting display — six LED strings, 100 LED C9 bulbs along the roofline, two inflatables and a projector — draws about 355 watts. At 6 hours a night for 40 days and 18¢ per kWh, that's about $15 for the season.

Old C9 Bulbs Are the Expensive Part

Classic incandescent C7 and C9 bulbs are the one decoration that can move a power bill. At 7 watts per bulb, 500 feet of C9s on 12-inch spacing draws 3,500 watts — more than two space heaters. Running 6 hours a night for 40 days, that's about $150 of electricity. The same roofline in LED C9s draws about 480 watts and costs about $21. If you still have incandescent big bulbs, swapping them pays back faster than any other holiday change.

The Department of Energy's older comparison makes the same point for a 6-foot tree lit 12 hours a day for 40 days: about $25 with C7 incandescent bulbs, $6 with mini incandescents and 56¢ with LEDs, at the 10.5¢ per kWh rates of the time.

Timers Cut the Bill in Half

Lights left on dusk to dawn run 14 hours or more in December. A timer or smart plug that shuts them off at midnight runs them about 6 hours, so it cuts the display's cost by more than half. It also keeps the outdoor plugs from running all night in rain and snow. Use outdoor-rated timers and cords, and plug outdoor decorations into GFCI-protected outlets.

Why the December Bill Still Jumps

If your December bill is up $40 or $80, lights are rarely the main reason unless you run a big incandescent display. Shorter days, more heating, holiday cooking, guests and space heaters usually add far more. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to run Christmas lights for a month?

An LED display of six 100-light strings and 100 roofline C9 bulbs uses about 145 watts, roughly $5 a month at 6 hours a night and 18¢ per kWh. The same display with incandescent bulbs uses about 940 watts, roughly $30 a month. Add about $3 a month for each 100-watt inflatable.

How much electricity does a Christmas inflatable use?

Most full-size inflatables draw roughly 50 to 250 watts for the blower and internal lights, with larger ones at the top of that range. Some newer models run from a 12-volt adapter and use under 20 watts. A 100-watt inflatable on for 6 hours a night costs about 11¢ a night, or $4 over a 40-day season.

Do LED Christmas lights use less electricity?

Yes, about 75% to 90% less. A 100-light LED string uses about 5 to 10 watts versus 40 watts for incandescent minis, and an LED C9 bulb about 1 watt versus 7 watts. For 500 feet of C9 roofline lights, that's about 480 watts instead of 3,500.

Is it expensive to leave Christmas lights on all night?

With LEDs, not very: a typical LED display costs a few cents more per night if left on dusk to dawn instead of 6 hours. With incandescent C9 bulbs it adds up quickly — 100 bulbs running 14 hours a night cost about $1.76 a night, compared with 76¢ on a 6-hour timer.

How much do Christmas lights add to your electric bill?

For most homes with LED lights and a few inflatables, about $5 to $20 for the season. Large incandescent displays can add $100 or more. Run the calculator above with your own strings, bulbs and inflatables to see your number.
